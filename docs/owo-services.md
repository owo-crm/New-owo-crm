## 1. How to interpret this catalogue

This document describes the services the company intends to offer. It does **not** mean that every service is already production-ready.

For each service, Claude and the team must distinguish:

- **Available now:** The team has the skills, equipment, capacity, and process to deliver it.
- **Available with a partner:** Delivery depends on an external specialist or supplier.
- **Planned:** The service is part of the intended offer but needs validation, training, equipment, or process development.
- **Custom scope:** Feasibility and price must be assessed individually.

Do not advertise a capability as available until the team has confirmed it.

## 2. Service architecture

The planned offer has five main pillars:

1. Social Media Management & Content Production.
2. Performance Marketing (Meta Ads and Google Ads).
3. Local SEO & Online Reputation.
4. Website Design & Development.
5. Business Automation & Custom Software.

These pillars can be sold individually or combined into a client-specific growth solution.

# A. Social Media Management & Content Production

## Objective

Help businesses build a consistent, professional social-media presence and turn relevant attention into enquiries, visits, bookings, and sales.

## Potential services

### Strategy and account setup
- Review of existing social-media profiles.
- Audience and competitor research.
- Platform selection based on the client's audience and objectives.
- Profile structure, descriptions, contact details, and calls to action.
- Content pillars and tone of voice.
- Monthly content strategy and calendar.

### Content production
- On-location photography.
- On-location video recording.
- Short-form vertical video for Reels, TikTok, and Shorts.
- Editing, subtitles, and basic motion graphics.
- Photo selection and retouching.
- Graphic posts, carousels, stories, and promotional assets.
- Copywriting and calls to action.
- Adaptation of assets to platform formats.

### Publishing and community support
- Scheduling and publishing approved content.
- Basic moderation and comment monitoring.
- Routing enquiries to the client's team.
- Preparing response templates where appropriate.
- Monthly performance review.

## Typical deliverables to define per contract

- Number of shoot sessions and their duration.
- Number of edited videos and photographs.
- Number and type of posts, stories, and short-form videos.
- Platforms covered.
- Copywriting and publishing responsibilities.
- Number of revision rounds.
- Approval deadlines and client responsibilities.
- Whether community management and direct-message handling are included.

Never imply unlimited content, unlimited revisions, or 24/7 inbox management unless explicitly contracted.

## Client value and KPIs

Potential KPIs:
- Consistency of publishing.
- Reach among relevant local audiences.
- Video watch time and completion.
- Engagement quality.
- Profile visits and link clicks.
- Enquiries, bookings, and sales attributable to social channels where tracking allows.

Follower growth and views should not be treated as the sole measure of success.

# B. Performance Marketing — Meta Ads & Google Ads

## Objective

Generate measurable demand and conversions through paid advertising, while managing the client's budget responsibly.

## Meta Ads

Potential work:
- Business Manager and ad-account review or setup.
- Pixel and Conversions API planning/implementation where appropriate.
- Audience and geographic targeting.
- Campaign structure and objective selection.
- Ad copy and creative concepts.
- Campaign launch and monitoring.
- Retargeting where lawful and technically appropriate.
- A/B testing.
- Budget pacing and optimisation.
- Reporting and recommendations.

## Google Ads

Potential work:
- Google Ads account review or setup.
- Keyword and search-intent research.
- Search campaigns.
- Display and remarketing campaigns where appropriate.
- Performance Max where appropriate for the business and available conversion data.
- Ad copy and asset preparation.
- Conversion tracking and analytics configuration.
- Search-term reviews and negative keywords.
- Budget and bid optimisation.
- Reporting and recommendations.

## Required measurement foundations

Before launch, clarify:
- The business objective and desired conversion.
- Target location and service area.
- Landing page or destination.
- Conversion tracking method.
- Lead-quality feedback from the client.
- Monthly media budget.
- Who owns the ad account and data.
- Who responds to incoming leads and how quickly.

The client's media spend should normally be separate from the agency management fee. State this clearly in proposals.

## KPIs

Depending on the campaign:
- Impressions and reach.
- Click-through rate (CTR).
- Cost per click (CPC).
- Conversion rate.
- Cost per lead or acquisition (CPL/CPA).
- Qualified leads.
- Bookings, purchases, and revenue.
- Return on ad spend (ROAS), when reliable revenue tracking exists.

Do not promise a specific ROAS, lead volume, or cost per lead without evidence. Explain the assumptions and uncertainty.

## Objective

Improve how easily local customers discover and evaluate the client's business through search engines, maps, and review platforms.

## Potential services

### Google Business Profile and local presence
- Profile setup or audit.
- Business category and service configuration.
- Opening hours, contact details, and service areas.
- Business description and relevant attributes.
- Photo and update recommendations.

### SEO

- Keyword and search-intent research.
- Local landing-page recommendations.

- Basic indexability and performance checks.
- Local competitor analysis.

- Ethical process for requesting genuine customer reviews.
- Monitoring and categorising reviews.
- Drafting professional responses.

- Calls, direction requests, and website visits.
- Relevant keyword trends.
- Genuine review volume and rating trends.

SEO is a long-term activity. Set expectations accordingly.

# D. Website Design & Development

## Objective

Create useful, credible, mobile-friendly websites that explain the client's offer and help visitors take the next step.

## Potential project types

- Landing pages.
- Business-card websites.
- Corporate and service websites.
- Product and brand websites.
- Online catalogues.
- E-commerce websites.
- Booking and appointment websites.
- Custom web applications.

## Typical project stages

1. Discovery: goals, audience, competitors, content, and requirements.
2. Scope: sitemap, functionality, integrations, and acceptance criteria.
3. Structure: user journeys and wireframes.
4. Design: visual direction and responsive layouts.
5. Development: implementation and integrations.
6. Content: text, images, and other supplied or produced assets.
7. QA: mobile, browser, form, performance, and basic accessibility checks.
8. Launch: domain, hosting, SSL, analytics, and deployment.
9. Handover: credentials, documentation, and training.
10. Maintenance: optional ongoing support.

## Baseline quality expectations

- Responsive design.
- Clear navigation and calls to action.
- Reasonable loading performance.
- Basic technical SEO foundations.
- Secure HTTPS configuration.
- Working forms and integrations.
- Analytics and consent configuration where required.
- Backups and update strategy appropriate to the platform.
- Clear ownership of domain, hosting, code, and accounts.

## Scope boundaries to define

Every proposal should state:
- Number of pages and templates.
- Included features and integrations.
- Who supplies copy, translations, photographs, and branding.
- Number of revision rounds.
- Hosting and domain responsibility.
- Third-party subscription costs.
- Maintenance and support terms.
- Ownership and handover arrangements.
- What is explicitly excluded.

# E. Business Automation & Custom Software

## Objective

Reduce repetitive work, improve visibility, and help clients manage selected business processes through suitable integrations, automations, and tailored software.

This is a potentially valuable differentiator, but custom development must be justified by a real operational problem.

## Potential solutions

### CRM and customer management
- Contact and company records.
- Lead pipeline and status tracking.
- Follow-up reminders.
- Notes and interaction history.
- Basic sales dashboards.
- Role-based access where needed.

### Orders and operations
- Order or job tracking.
- Booking and appointment workflows.
- Task assignment.
- Status dashboards.
- Internal notifications.
- Simple approval processes.

### Inventory and business administration
- Product and stock tracking.
- Low-stock notifications.
- Basic purchasing workflows.
- Operational reports.
- Data entry and export tools.

### Team workflows
- Employee or contractor records.
- Shift and availability workflows.
- Task checklists.
- Internal announcements.
- Basic time or activity tracking where appropriate and lawful.

### Integrations and microservices
- Google Sheets integrations.
- Email notifications.
- Telegram notifications or bots.
- Forms and lead capture.
- External API integrations.
- Data synchronisation between tools.
- Small internal dashboards and admin panels.

## Delivery approach

1. Understand the existing process.
2. Identify the specific bottleneck or business cost.
3. Map the current workflow.
4. Check whether an existing product or no-code tool is sufficient.
5. Define a minimum viable solution.
6. Estimate implementation and ongoing support costs.
7. Build and test with representative users and data.
8. Document, hand over, and agree maintenance.

Do not build custom software merely because it is technically possible. Prefer the simplest reliable solution that meets the client's needs.

## Technical and operational requirements

Depending on the project, consider:
- Authentication and access control.
- Least-privilege permissions.
- Data minimisation.
- GDPR/RODO roles and responsibilities.
- Secure secrets and API-key handling.
- Backups and restore testing.
- Logging and error monitoring.
- Hosting, uptime, and maintenance.
- Data export and exit arrangements.
- Documentation and ownership of source code.

Avoid storing sensitive customer data unless necessary and properly protected. Do not promise legal compliance solely because a technical feature has been implemented.

## 4. Packaging and pricing principles

No prices have been approved yet.

When developing pricing:
- Calculate delivery hours and realistic capacity.
- Include preparation, communication, revisions, travel, reporting, and project management.
- Account for software, equipment, contractors, taxes, and overhead.
- Separate one-off setup fees from recurring service fees.
- Separate advertising spend and third-party costs.
- Define minimum engagement periods where appropriate.
- Set boundaries on revisions, content volume, response times, and support.
- Check that the price produces an acceptable contribution margin.

Quote in PLN and label amounts as netto or brutto. Confirm VAT treatment with the company's accountant or qualified adviser.

## 5. Client onboarding requirements

Before starting work, collect the information relevant to the project:

- Company name and legal/business details.
- Main contact and decision-maker.
- Business location and service area.
- Products or services and pricing.
- Target customer.
- Main business objective.
- Current website and social profiles.
- Existing advertising and analytics accounts.
- Brand assets and content permissions.
- Competitors or reference examples.
- Budget, deadlines, and constraints.
- Who approves work and who handles incoming leads.

Use a written brief and agreed scope. Obtain appropriate permissions and account access securely; do not ask clients to send passwords in plain text.

## 6. Reporting and client communication

Reports should connect activity to the agreed business objective.

A useful monthly report may include:
1. Objectives and work completed.
2. Key results and trends.
3. Leads, bookings, sales, or other business outcomes where measurable.
4. Spend and cost metrics where relevant.
5. What worked and what did not.
6. Recommended next actions.
7. Dependencies or decisions needed from the client.

Be clear about attribution limitations. Do not claim that the agency caused a result when the available data cannot support that conclusion.

## 7. Service exclusions and safeguards

Unless separately agreed, do not assume the following are included:
- Advertising media spend.
- Influencer fees or paid placements.
- Professional models, actors, locations, or props.
- Printing and physical production.
- Premium fonts, stock assets, plugins, and software licences.
- Legal advice, accounting, or formal GDPR consultancy.
- 24/7 technical support.
- Unlimited revisions or content requests.
- Guaranteed sales, rankings, leads, or revenue.
- Customer service or sales closing on behalf of the client.

For regulated sectors or sensitive claims, obtain appropriate client approvals and specialist review.

## 9. Guidance for Claude

When asked to design, price, sell, or deliver a service:

1. Identify the client's actual business problem.
2. Confirm which capability is genuinely available.
3. Recommend the smallest effective scope.
4. Define deliverables, dependencies, exclusions, and success metrics.
5. Estimate effort and costs before proposing a price.
6. Identify operational, technical, legal, and reputational risks.
7. Avoid guaranteeing outcomes outside the agency's control.
8. Recommend a partner or decline the work if the team cannot deliver it responsibly.

Use this catalogue as a service framework. Update it when the team validates capabilities, launches new services, changes delivery standards, or approves pricing.
